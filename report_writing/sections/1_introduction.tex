\scaffold{Bullets to expand into prose. Existing \% comments below give the intended narrative order.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item RBPs drive post-transcriptional regulation; mapping their binding sites genome-wide is central to RNA biology. eCLIP yields aligned reads, so a \emph{peak caller} is required to turn reads into discrete binding sites.
  \item PureCLIP's output is highly sensitive to a few numeric parameters, and there is no universal optimum --- the best values depend on RBP, cell line and antibody efficiency.
  \item Manual tuning does not scale (hours per genome-wide run, subjective, dozens of ENCODE datasets) --- motivating an automated, reproducible framework.
  \item Contribution: an automated \emph{propose $\rightarrow$ run $\rightarrow$ score $\rightarrow$ iterate} framework with two interchangeable optimizers on identical evaluation infrastructure --- an LLM agent (biological priors + reasoning) vs.\ Optuna/TPE (black-box Bayesian search).
  \item Scope: the study grew from a 3-RBP, crisp-motif pilot (RBFOX2, QKI, PUM1) to \textbf{26 datasets across 12+ RBPs}, adding a class of \emph{splicing factors} whose motifs are degenerate or positional --- a deliberate stress-test of generalization.
  \item Research question: does LLM reasoning add value over principled numerical optimization for a small bioinformatics parameter space --- and \emph{does that value depend on how hard the RBP's motif is}?
