Enhanced crosslinking and immunoprecipitation (eCLIP) maps RNA-binding protein
(RBP) contacts transcriptome-wide, but converting aligned reads into binding
sites requires parameterised peak calling. We developed an automated framework
that optimizes seven PureCLIP and post-processing parameters against a composite
of replicate reproducibility, motif support, and reference-region recall. A
large language model (LLM) agent and a Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE)
use the same pipeline, parameter bounds, and objective; a yield penalty excludes
degenerate solutions containing fewer than ten sites. We evaluated the framework
on chromosome~21 in 12 completed paired LLM--TPE dataset/cell-line comparisons
across K562 and HepG2, with six additional K562 prior ablations. TPE produced the
higher observed best score in ten pairs. However, TPE runs used 14--16
evaluations and LLM runs only five to seven, precluding a controlled efficiency
ranking. Prior and no-prior LLM outcomes were nearly even overall: the mean
prior advantage was 0.004 and the median difference was $-0.002$, with modest
dataset-specific variation except for HNRNPK. The proposed monotonic relation
between motif difficulty and prior benefit was not observed. These results establish a
reproducible evaluation framework and an empirical baseline for a subset of the
larger dataset registry; replicated, equal-budget experiments are required to
compare optimizers and determine when
biological context improves peak calling.
