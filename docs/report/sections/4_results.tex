\section{Results}
\label{results}

The principal comparison comprises nine optimizer--dataset combinations across
PUM2, HNRNPK, and U2AF2. Eight completed normally; U2AF2 TPE ended after two
evaluations because the batch reached its time limit. QKI and PUM1 are reported
separately as pilots. Every value is the best composite score observed in one
chromosome-21 run and should be interpreted as a point estimate.

\subsection{Optimization gains depended on the dataset}

Table~\ref{tab:h2h} compares each search with the shared default configuration.
For PUM2, all three searches improved the default score of 0.260, with gains from
0.092 to 0.124. HNRNPK also improved under the prior-equipped LLM ($+0.087$) and
TPE ($+0.133$), while the no-prior LLM returned to the default as its best
configuration. In contrast, the prior-equipped LLM did not improve the U2AF2
default. The no-prior arm improved it by 0.037. The incomplete TPE run found a
score of 0.488 in its second evaluation, but it cannot be treated as a converged
result.

The pilots show the same dataset dependence. On QKI, the LLM arms improved the
default by 0.029--0.039, whereas TPE improved it by only 0.001. Neither completed
search improved the PUM1 default; the reported TPE value is additionally affected
by the yield penalty because its best trial contained seven sites.

\subsection{The prior ablation did not follow a monotonic difficulty trend}

The difference between the two LLM arms was $-0.031$ for PUM2, $+0.087$ for
HNRNPK, and $-0.037$ for U2AF2. Thus, the biological prior was associated with a
higher score only for HNRNPK. This pattern does not support the proposed monotonic
relationship in which prior benefit increases from crisp to degenerate to
positional binding. It is instead consistent with a dataset-specific effect, but
one dataset per motif class is insufficient to establish a class-level
relationship.

Figure~\ref{fig:convergence} shows that the HNRNPK difference emerged within two
LLM updates. The no-prior arm explored four alternatives without exceeding the
default, whereas the prior-equipped arm reached 0.329. PUM2 shows the opposite
pattern: the no-prior LLM achieved the highest score among the three arms. For
U2AF2, the prior-equipped LLM retained the default while the no-prior arm improved
after changing merge distance and minimum crosslink support.

\subsection{The available protocol does not identify a superior optimizer}

TPE exceeded the prior-equipped LLM by 0.019 on PUM2 and 0.046 on HNRNPK. It
also produced the highest observed U2AF2 score, although that run was incomplete.
Conversely, the prior-equipped LLM exceeded TPE by 0.038 in the completed QKI
pilot. The PUM1 comparison is not interpretable because the TPE run timed out and
its best trial fell below the ten-site threshold.

These differences do not constitute a controlled ranking. Completed TPE runs
evaluated approximately twice as many configurations as the LLM runs, and two
TPE runs were truncated by wall-clock limits. The results demonstrate that both
strategies can locate improvements, but an equal-budget replicated experiment is
needed to compare their efficiency and final performance.

\subsection{Metric decomposition and decision trace}

The best HNRNPK LLM configuration increased replicate reproducibility from 0.273
to 0.435 and motif hit rate from 0.273 to 0.348, while reference recall decreased
from 0.15 to 0.10 (Table~\ref{tab:bio}). Its first proposal invoked HNRNPK's
C-rich motif to reduce bandwidth from 50 to 30 nt and simultaneously increased
merge distance. That combined proposal initially lowered the composite score.
The following proposal retained the narrower bandwidth, restored the merge
distance, and increased the minimum crosslink count; this configuration achieved
the reported maximum. The trace therefore shows that the prior changed the
agent's search rationale, but it does not isolate bandwidth as the causal source
of improvement.

Across the representative best trials, motif enrichment was highest for PUM2 and
lowest for U2AF2. U2AF2 instead exhibited high reference recall and a much larger
site count. These differences illustrate why the composite must be decomposed:
similar aggregate scores can represent distinct biological and computational
trade-offs.

An exploratory inspection of decision traces compared the compact prompt with
the more detailed development variant. In both cases, individual proposals were
not always consistent with the reported score components. In particular, the
model occasionally increased filtering stringency after evaluations with few
retained sites and little motif support. Subsequent proposals sometimes returned
to configurations with higher site yield. These observations concern the
sequence of parameter proposals rather than the objective itself: low-yield call
sets are down-weighted by the yield guard and may also have lower reference
recall. No systematic difference between the two prompts was established. The
target-gene information supplied by the detailed prompt was not evident in the
rationales, although the optimization feedback contained no peak-to-gene
annotations with which to evaluate or apply that information.
